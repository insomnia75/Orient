          機構

          How it ticks

          There is no battery inside an Orient automatic. A spring stores the energy, a gear train spends it, and a tiny wheel decides how fast time goes by.

          Six beats a second

          The balance wheel swings back and forth on a hairspring. Every swing lets the escape wheel advance one small step, and the hands move on. Orient’s calibres make 21,600 of these half-swings an hour, which is six a second, or three full oscillations.

          The drawing is slowed to a tenth. At real speed it is a blur.

          It winds itself

          A half-disc weight, the rotor, swings with every movement of your wrist and winds the mainspring through a gear. After roughly 40 hours without wearing it, the spring runs down and the watch stops.

          Orient’s F6 calibres can also be wound by hand with the crown, and they hack: pulling the crown stops the seconds hand so you can set the time to the second.

          Made in Japan, in house

          Orient says it develops and makes its own mechanical movements in Japan. They are built by Akita Epson, a company in the Epson group, in Yuzawa, in the north of Honshu.

            制作

            About this project

            A concept site for Orient mechanical watches, made as a portfolio piece. It is not affiliated with Orient, and nothing on it is for sale.

            The brief

            Build a watch site that feels like a calm, precise object instead of a template: one model you can turn and take apart, a collection, a history, and a page that explains how the mechanism works.

            Orient was a good subject. The range runs from a slim dress watch to a diver to a moon-phase, the company has a documented history in Japan, and the Japanese vocabulary of watchmaking gave the design a point of view.

            What is in it

            • Home has a slow watch movement in the hero and the 3D stage: five models in fourteen colourways, hotspots on each part, an exploded view and a specifications drawer.
            • Collection filters by series and compares the models in one table. Each watch is drawn at its real size relative to the others.
            • Model pages give each watch its own address, with a live face that keeps the time, a story, an anatomy in Japanese and English, and specifications.
            • Heritage is twelve dated entries from 1901 to 2025. A train of wheels turns as you scroll.
            • Craft shows a balance wheel and a rotor at work, with a glossary of ten Japanese terms.

            Stack

            HTML, hand-written CSS with design tokens, and JavaScript modules loaded straight by the browser, with no bundler. three.js draws the 3D, GSAP and ScrollTrigger drive the timeline and reveals, and Lenis handles smooth scrolling. Fonts are Shippori Mincho and Zen Kaku Gothic New.

            A Tailwind config and build script are included for anyone who wants utility classes. The pages shipped here use plain CSS, because the Tailwind browser build added weight the site did not need.

            Decisions worth a look

            One document, hash routes
            Five views share a page and switch on the URL hash. It runs on any static host. The 3D code downloads only when the home view opens, so a link to the collection never pays for it.
            One gear, drawn once
            A single outline function makes every wheel, in the 3D hero and in the SVG on the Heritage page. Meshing wheels are phased from their tooth counts, and a test samples the train at several moments to confirm no teeth overlap.
            Slow on purpose
            The barrel turns slowly and the faster wheels follow from the tooth counts, so no tooth jumps more than a fraction of its pitch between frames. The balance wheel is shown in slow motion, because at its real six beats a second it would only blur.
            Every watch is drawn from data
            There are no image files. The 3D model and the 2D drawing on cards and model pages are both built from the same measurements in millimetres: case diameter, lug width, lug to lug, bezel type, strap. A new entry gets its strap, lugs, crown, bezel and dial without any artwork.
            It degrades instead of breaking
            Without WebGL the stage draws the 2D watch with live hands. If the 3D libraries fail to load, every other page still works. When frames run long, the renderer lowers its pixel ratio.
            Content is data
            Models live in one file and the history in another. Add an entry and the rail, filters, pages, table and drawer follow.

            Adding a model

            Everything a model needs sits in one entry in src/data/watches.js. Set image to a photograph and the collection card and model page use it in place of the drawn watch.

            {
  id: 'kamasu',
  name: 'Kamasu',
  series: 'sport',
  jp: 'カマス',
  image: '/photos/kamasu.jpg',      // optional, replaces the drawn watch
  gallery: [{ src: '/photos/kamasu-side.jpg', alt: 'Side view' }],
  case: { diameter: 41.8, thickness: 12.8, metal: '#c3c8ce' },
  variants: [{ id: 'green', name: 'Green', dial: '#1f5a46' }],
  specs: [{ label: 'Movement', value: 'Calibre F6922' }],
}

            What is placeholder

            • The watches are drawn in code. There is no product photography, so dials, hands and straps are illustrations of the real models, not official artwork.
            • Specifications come from public listings and should be checked against the current Orient range before anyone relies on them.
            • History comes from the public sources listed at the foot of the Heritage page.
            • The newsletter form checks the address and sends nothing.
            • Orient and the model names belong to their owners. This is an unofficial study.
